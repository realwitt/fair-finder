Fair Finder Unofficial guide

Find any food at the 2026 N.C. State Fair, then walk straight to it.

A free map for your phone. Type what you’re craving and see every booth that lists it, nearest walk first.

Open the app Works in your phone’s browser. Nothing to install, no account.

What it does

Search any food

Search by what’s on the menu, not just by vendor name. “Turkey leg”, “funnel cake” or “lemonade” finds every booth whose fair listing mentions it, with close matches and common spellings covered.

Live walking directions

Tap a booth and follow the arrow. It turns with your phone’s compass, a route is drawn along the fairground paths, and hints like “In 180 ft, bear right” update as you walk. If you wander off, it reroutes.

Find an ATM

Turn on the ATM layer to see cash machines around the grounds, taken from the fair’s published grounds map. Their spots are approximate, so look for signs as you get close.

Share a booth link

Found the good corn dogs? Tap Share to send a link. Your friend opens it and gets walking directions to that booth from wherever they are.

Popular booths show under the search box when several devices have walked up to them or tapped the same food. Counts are anonymous, and a booth or food appears only after at least 5 different devices. Turn sharing off in the app’s Layers panel.

How to use it

  1. Allow locationWhen your browser asks, tap Allow so the map can show where you are. On iPhone, if you tapped Don’t Allow, go to Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › Safari Websites.
  2. Tap “Enable compass”Start guiding to a booth and tap the 🧭 button so the arrow turns with your phone. Without it, the arrow points north-up until you start walking.
  3. Add to Home ScreeniPhone (Safari): tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Android (Chrome): tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app. It opens full screen like an app.

Use with your AI

Fair Finder also runs a small, read-only MCP server, so AI assistants that support remote MCP connectors can search the same booth, food and ride data in a chat (for example “where can I get a cherry slush?” or “where’s the Tilt-A-Whirl?”). Every answer includes a Fair Finder link that opens walking directions on your phone. It is unofficial, like the app.

Server URL: https://fair-finder-mcp.lively-shape-f373.workers.dev/mcp

  1. ClaudeSettings › Connectors › Add custom connector. Paste the server URL and choose “No sign in”.
  2. ChatGPTTurn on developer mode (Settings › Apps & Connectors › Advanced), then create an app or connector with the server URL and “No Authentication”.
  3. Other MCP clientsAdd a remote (streamable HTTP) MCP server with the URL above. No API key or account is needed. Tools: search, get_details, nearby.

Accuracy

Booth locations and menus come from the fair’s published data and may change during the fair. ATM locations are approximate. Walking routes come from OpenStreetMap and don’t reflect temporary booths, closures or crowds. Follow posted signs and staff directions; use at your own risk. Provided as-is, with no warranty.

Privacy

Your location is used only on your device to show where you are and guide you. It is never sent to us or stored on a server. Unless you opt out, the app sends a random device id created on this device (the server stores only a salted hash of it), the booth id when your GPS shows you arriving within about 50 ft of a booth, and the food chip or booth you pick (never what you type). Your IP address is used only as a salted, daily-rotated hash for rate limiting; raw IP addresses are not stored, and those rate-limit rows are deleted in about 2 hours. Visit and tap rows are deleted after 3 days. A booth or search appears only after at least 5 different devices. No accounts, no ads, no tracking cookies. The app saves the booth you’re guiding to in your browser so it can resume, and whether the guide panel was open. Favorites are stored only on this device (localStorage) and are never sent anywhere. Opt out in the Layers panel, or clear this site's data in your browser (which also resets the device id). The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which logs visitor IP addresses for security. Map images load from OpenStreetMap, Esri and the fair’s ArcGIS server, which see normal web requests. The optional AI connector (MCP server, hosted on Cloudflare Workers) serves only the same public booth and ride data and is read-only: no accounts, and no logging of what you search. To limit abuse it counts requests per minute using a salted, one-way hash of your IP address; raw IP addresses are not stored. Cloudflare sees normal web requests to the counter.
